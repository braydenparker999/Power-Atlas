# Contributing to Power Atlas

Power Atlas welcomes contributions from researchers, developers, historians, journalists, students, and curious members of the public.

The project is designed around **inspectable evidence**, not ideological agreement.

## Contribution types

You can contribute by:

- adding or correcting entities
- adding sourced relationships
- improving dates and chronology
- attaching stronger sources to existing claims
- challenging weak or misleading edges
- improving software and visualization
- building importers for compatible public datasets
- helping with entity deduplication
- documenting licensing/provenance for external datasets

## Required for factual additions

A proposed factual claim should include:

1. the exact claim
2. the source URL or archival citation
3. the relevant date or date range
4. a short excerpt or location reference when practical
5. an evidence grade proposal
6. any important limitation or ambiguity

## AI-assisted contributions

AI-assisted work is allowed.

However:

- AI output is not a source.
- AI-generated citations must be independently checked.
- Fabricated or unverifiable citations are grounds for rejection.
- If AI materially assisted the research, extraction, translation, or drafting, disclosure is encouraged.
- The same evidence standard applies whether the contribution began with AI or without it.

The project is intentionally tool-agnostic: credibility comes from reproducible evidence and review.

## Review philosophy

Reviewers should ask:

- Does the source actually support the claim?
- Are the dates aligned?
- Is the relationship label more precise than a vague `connected to` edge?
- Does the edge imply more than the source proves?
- Is contradictory evidence missing?
- Is the source primary when a primary record is reasonably available?

## No guilt by association

A relationship may be important without implying wrongdoing. Contributors must avoid wording that converts proximity into culpability without separate evidence.

## Disputes

Disputes should focus on evidence, wording, dates, provenance, and inference strength. Political or ideological disagreement alone is not a reason to remove a documented fact.

## Sensitive personal attributes

For living people, religion, ethnicity, ancestry, political identity, and similar sensitive attributes require strong public documentation and contextual relevance. Do not infer them from names, appearance, stereotypes, or demographic assumptions.

For historical figures, documented historical inference can be included with an explicit basis and confidence level.

## Community goal

The ideal contribution makes it easier for someone who disagrees with you to independently verify what you added.
