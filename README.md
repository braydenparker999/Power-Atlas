# Power Atlas

Power Atlas is an evidence-first, time-aware map of relationships among people, families, institutions, companies, governments, foundations, media organizations, universities, and other centers of influence.

The goal is not to enforce a mainstream narrative or a contrarian one. The goal is to make claims inspectable.

## Core principle

Every meaningful relationship should answer four questions:

1. **What is the relationship?**
2. **When did it exist?**
3. **What evidence supports it?**
4. **What does that evidence *not* establish?**

Power Atlas should make it easy for a casual user to explore a network and equally easy for a serious researcher to open the underlying evidence.

## Planned views

- Interactive network graph
- Historical time slider
- Article/profile view
- Evidence drawer for every relationship
- Shortest documented path between two entities
- Filters for family, banking, government, philanthropy, media, academia, defense/intelligence, religion/background, ownership, appointments, donations, and other relationship types

## Evidence grades

- **A — Primary evidence:** original filing, contract, archival document, government record, court record, contemporaneous correspondence, official register, etc.
- **B — Strongly corroborated:** multiple high-quality independent sources or a reliable scholarly reconstruction grounded in primary material.
- **C — Credible secondary evidence:** a solid source, but not independently corroborated yet.
- **D — Inference:** plausible interpretation that must be displayed as inference, never as established fact.
- **E — Unverified:** lead or claim retained for investigation but excluded from the canonical graph by default.

## AI policy

AI may assist with software development, entity extraction, source discovery, deduplication, transcription, translation, and research triage.

**AI output is never evidence by itself.** Canonical claims must be traceable to inspectable sources. AI-assisted additions must meet the same sourcing and review requirements as human-authored additions.

This project will document where automated systems are used rather than pretending they are absent.

## Community model

The long-term model is community-edited, but not instant-publish. Contributions become proposed claims or edges, with source evidence attached. Review determines whether they enter the canonical graph.

Contributor reputation should reflect the durability and sourcing quality of accepted contributions, not popularity or ideology.

## Current status

Early prototype and schema design.

The first research module will focus on historical banking, finance, government, and family networks before expanding into other domains.

## Data sources

Potential sources include Wikidata, LittleSis, ICIJ Offshore Leaks, public corporate registries, SEC filings, campaign-finance records, nonprofit filings, parliamentary/congressional records, archival collections, court documents, historical newspapers, and scholarly research.

External datasets will only be imported after their licensing and provenance requirements are documented.

## Important distinction

A documented relationship does not automatically establish coordination, control, wrongdoing, ideological agreement, or causation. Power Atlas should make those distinctions visible instead of leaving users to infer them from a line on a graph.
