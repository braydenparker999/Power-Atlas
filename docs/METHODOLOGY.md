# Power Atlas Methodology

## 1. Facts before narratives

Power Atlas stores discrete claims rather than broad conclusions whenever possible.

Example:

- Good claim: `Henry P. Davison attended the Jekyll Island meeting in November 1910.`
- Separate claim: `Davison was a partner at J. P. Morgan & Co.`
- Not automatically implied: `J. P. Morgan personally directed the meeting.`

Each claim must stand on its own evidence.

## 2. Relationship model

Every relationship should include:

- source entity
- target entity
- relationship type
- direction, if applicable
- start date or best-supported date range
- end date, if applicable
- geographic scope when relevant
- source citation(s)
- evidence grade
- short factual summary
- explicit limitations: what the evidence does not establish
- contributor/reviewer history

## 3. Evidence grades

### A — Primary evidence
Original records such as government filings, contracts, court records, corporate registers, archival correspondence, contemporaneous minutes, tax/nonprofit filings, electoral records, or other first-party documents.

### B — Strongly corroborated
Multiple high-quality independent sources, or a scholarly reconstruction that directly cites strong primary material.

### C — Credible secondary evidence
A reputable single secondary source that has not yet been independently corroborated.

### D — Inference
A conclusion drawn from established facts but not directly documented. D-grade material must be visually distinct and cannot silently become factual narrative.

### E — Unverified lead
A claim worth checking. E-grade material is investigative workspace material and is excluded from default public canonical views.

## 4. Source diversity

No publication, government, archive, academic institution, activist organization, corporation, or independent outlet is treated as inherently infallible.

Where a claim is contested, reviewers should seek the strongest primary evidence and materially different interpretations. Source diversity is useful when it improves evidentiary coverage, not as a mechanical requirement to create artificial balance.

## 5. Sensitive attributes

Religion, ethnicity, ancestry, political identity, and similar attributes must never be inferred from appearance or surname alone for living people.

For living people, publish these attributes only when reliably self-identified or strongly documented in reputable public sources and relevant to the research context.

For historical/deceased figures, historical inference may be recorded when necessary, but the basis and confidence level must be explicit.

A shared ethnicity, religion, school, neighborhood, or family origin is not itself evidence of coordination.

## 6. Time matters

Every edge should be temporal whenever the evidence allows it. A board membership in 1985 must not imply collaboration with someone who joined the same board in 2015.

The public graph should default to date-aware rendering.

## 7. AI-assisted research

AI may:

- identify candidate entities and relationships
- summarize documents for triage
- help write importers and software
- suggest duplicate matches
- translate or transcribe sources
- propose source classifications

AI may not serve as the source for a canonical factual claim.

Every AI-assisted claim must resolve to inspectable evidence that a human reviewer can independently verify.

When AI materially assisted a contribution, contributors should be encouraged to disclose that fact. The project judges claims by evidence quality, not by whether a human or tool first noticed them.

## 8. Corrections

Corrections should be visible, attributable, and reversible. Removing a claim does not erase its revision history.

Serious factual challenges should attach directly to the disputed node, edge, or source rather than becoming general ideological arguments.

## 9. Discovery standard

Power Atlas distinguishes:

- new to the contributor
- rarely discussed
- apparently undocumented in existing literature
- demonstrably novel

Claims of novelty require a documented search for prior scholarship/reporting.

## 10. Core editorial rule

A line on the graph means only what its relationship label and supporting evidence say it means.

It does not automatically mean control, conspiracy, friendship, ideological agreement, wrongdoing, causation, or coordinated intent.
